## Vector DB Use Case

A traditional keyword search alone will not reliably satisfy lawyers querying 500‑page contracts in plain English. Keyword search (even with fuzzy matching) operates on literal terms, so it often misses passages that use different wording for the same idea—e.g., “termination” vs. “ending this agreement for cause,” or “early exit” scattered across cross‑references (“see Section 12.4”). It also struggles when key concepts are spread across multiple sections, when synonyms or paraphrases are used, or when the lawyer’s question doesn’t contain the exact index terms. The result is high effort (many reformulations), lower recall, and the risk of overlooking critical clauses.

A vector database addresses these limitations by storing dense vector embeddings of clause‑ or paragraph‑level chunks. Embeddings capture **semantic meaning**, not just surface words. At query time, the user’s question is embedded, and the system retrieves top‑k semantically similar chunks via approximate nearest‑neighbor (ANN) search. This naturally surfaces relevant sections that never share exact keywords, improving recall on nuanced legal phrasing. In practice, the pipeline also includes text **chunking** (e.g., 500–1,000 tokens with overlap), **metadata filters** (party names, dates, jurisdiction) to narrow scope, and a **hybrid search** step that blends BM25 (for precision on exact terms) with vectors (for semantic coverage). A lightweight re‑ranker can further sort the top hits.

In this system, the vector DB powers fast ANN retrieval over millions of contract chunks, while the document store remains the source of truth for rendering text and citations. Together, they reduce review time, improve answer quality, and minimize the chance of missing consequential obligations or termination conditions.
